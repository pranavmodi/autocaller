# PI Firm Persona Inventory

*Created 2026-06-12. Seed document for a future PI-firm mindset brief. This file
only defines the persona map; the next pass should capture each persona's
mindset, fears, goals, objections, trusted metrics, vocabulary, and buying role.*

## Purpose

Possible Minds sells operational AI/software to personal-injury firms. Outreach,
discovery, product bets, and listening-system synthesis should not treat "PI
firm" as one buyer. A firm is a collection of operators with different incentives:
owners care about signed cases and profit, intake cares about speed and handoff,
case staff care about status visibility and fewer follow-up loops, and finance
cares about cash recovery and leakage.

This inventory is the canonical first-pass list of personas to model.

## Persona Groups

### 1. Ownership and Executive Decision Makers

| Persona | Common titles | Buying role | Why they matter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder / owner | Founder, owner, principal, shareholder | Economic buyer | Owns growth, profitability, reputation, risk, and final approval in many small/mid-size firms. |
| Managing partner | Managing partner, senior partner | Economic buyer / champion | Balances caseload, attorney productivity, staffing, and vendor decisions. Often the main buyer for operations tooling. |
| Executive director / COO | COO, executive director, director of operations | Operational buyer / champion | Owns day-to-day throughput, staffing, process, reporting, and implementation success. |
| CEO / president | CEO, president | Economic buyer | More common in scaled PI firms with business-operator structure. Focuses on growth engine, margins, and management leverage. |
| Practice group leader | PI practice chair, litigation lead, pre-litigation lead | Influencer / workflow owner | Controls how a specific legal workflow operates and whether staff actually adopt a tool. |
| Non-attorney business partner | Business manager, operating partner | Economic buyer / evaluator | May own vendor selection, marketing spend, call center, analytics, or acquisition strategy. |

## Cross-Persona Motivation: The Champion Dynamic (added 2026-06-12)

For every non-owner persona (groups 3-6, 8, and non-partner attorneys), two
motivations exist that have nothing to do with buying: **make my own day
easier** and **look informed and ahead of the curve to my boss and peers**.
Staff who engage with AI vendors are often doing identity work — being the
person at the firm who knows what's coming. Outreach to these personas
should serve those motivations directly: frame around their personal chase,
and give them a forwardable artifact that earns them credit upstairs. This
fills the template rows below for staff personas: *who they need to
convince* = their boss; *what proof changes their mind* = something they
can deliver upward and look good doing it. Operationalized in the composer
skill as the `champion_enablement` strategy.

## Suggested Mindset Template For Each Persona

Use this structure when expanding the brief:

- What they are trying to accomplish
- What makes their day painful
- What they are afraid will break
- What they believe about AI/software vendors
- What metrics they trust
- What vocabulary sounds native to them
- What proof would change their mind
- What they can approve directly
- Who they need to convince
- Best opening wedge
- Likely objections
- Discovery questions that earn trust
