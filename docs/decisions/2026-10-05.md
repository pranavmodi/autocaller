# Decisions — 2026-10-05

## D-2026-10-05-01 — Persist legal-degree eligibility on job postings
- **Area:** product
- **Status:** accepted
- **Decision:** Classify whether each job requires a law degree or attorney license once with batched TypeSafe Jev, persist the judgment and provenance, and keep unclear roles visible; Leads hides only required roles by default.
- **Why:** Attorney categories alone miss non-attorney titles that mandate a JD, while classifying during each filter query would be slow, costly, and inconsistent.
- **Refs:** app/services/job_legal_degree_classification.py frontend/app/emailtag-firms/page.tsx

## D-2026-10-05-02 — Guard legal-degree judgments with source candidate evidence
- **Area:** product
- **Status:** accepted
- **Decision:** Use a mechanical credential/title prefilter only to decide which postings reach Jev; Jev remains the final semantic decision. Postings without a legal-credential candidate remain unclear and visible, preventing unrelated roles from being hidden.
- **Why:** The first live backfill showed Jev false positives on unrelated roles with no legal credential evidence. The prefilter reduces cost and blocks those false positives without using string matching as the final semantic decision.
- **Refs:** supersedes D-2026-10-05-01 in part; app/services/job_legal_degree_classification.py

## D-2026-10-05-03 — Resolve conflicting job work-arrangement evidence with Jev
- **Area:** product
- **Status:** accepted
- **Decision:** Keep mechanical remote/hybrid/onsite terms only as a candidate prefilter; when a posting contains conflicting or negated arrangement evidence, TypeSafe Jev makes and persists the final remote, hybrid, onsite, or unclear judgment.
- **Why:** The legacy first-match rule labeled hybrid schedules and even explicit no-remote listings as remote. A semantic conflict judgment handles mixed alternatives and required office attendance without hard-coded precedence.
- **Refs:** app/services/job_work_arrangement_classification.py
