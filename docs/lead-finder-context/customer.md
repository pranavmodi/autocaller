# Customer

> **Status:** First draft for the Possible Minds lead-finder agent  
> **Last grounded:** 2026-08-23  
> **Scope:** Initial lead-finder loop for US personal injury firms

## Ideal customer profile

### Primary ICP

A plaintiff-side PI firm where client acquisition is expensive, operational
volume is high enough for delay or inconsistency to compound, and leadership is
willing to improve one measurable workflow without surrendering human judgment.

Strong indicators include:

- meaningful inbound lead volume from paid search, television, referrals, web
  forms, calls, or multiple offices;
- auto-accident, catastrophic-injury, or another PI case mix with valuable,
  time-sensitive inquiries;
- roughly 5–30 attorneys or equivalent operating scale as a useful starting
  heuristic, not a hard gate;
- a dedicated intake, marketing, operations, records, case-management, or lien
  function;
- use of a modern intake, CRM, case-management, phone, or call-tracking system;
- visible pressure around after-hours coverage, follow-up, hiring, handoffs,
  case velocity, client updates, or AI governance; and
- an identifiable owner for the workflow and a practical metric the firm could
  improve.

### Best people to recommend

**Economic buyers and sponsors**

- Founder, owner, principal, CEO, or managing partner
- COO, executive director, director of operations, or firm administrator
- Intake director, call-center leader, or head of new-client acquisition
- CMO, marketing director, growth leader, or intake-analytics owner
- Legal-operations, systems, or innovation leader in a larger firm

**Workflow champions and pain witnesses**

- Intake manager or specialist
- New-client or sign-up coordinator
- Case manager or client-services leader
- Records manager, paralegal, or demand-preparation lead
- Lien negotiator, settlement coordinator, or disbursement lead
- Case-management system administrator

The lead finder must label the person's likely role: **buyer**, **sponsor**,
**champion**, **pain witness**, or **market expert**. Do not give a staff member
the buying authority of a partner merely because they are easier to find.

### Secondary and research-only targets

PI consultants, intake consultants, legal-marketing operators, case-management
implementers, medical lien providers, records vendors, and podcast hosts can be
valuable market experts or channels. They should be recommended as such, not
misrepresented as PI-firm buyers.

Healthcare or other regulated operators require explicit user approval before
entering the initial lead queue.

## Pains

### Tier 1: intake revenue leakage

- Missed calls and after-hours inquiries
- Slow first response while a prospect is contacting other firms
- Inconsistent qualification or fact capture
- Leads waiting in inboxes, voicemail, or disconnected queues
- Weak or inconsistent follow-up after the first unsuccessful attempt
- Broken handoffs between marketing, answering services, intake staff, and
  lawyers
- Retainers sent but not completed
- Poor visibility from lead source to meaningful contact, qualification, and
  signed case

### Tier 2: case and firm operations

- Medical-records, bills, treatment updates, and authorizations that must be
  chased manually
- Missing information that delays demand readiness
- Routine status calls and inconsistent client updates
- Liens, provider follow-up, reductions, and settlement-to-disbursement delay
- High-volume email routing and repetitive responses
- Work that depends on staff memory or one heroic operator
- Weak visibility into aging cases, exceptions, and work that needs attention

### Tier 3: AI adoption and risk

- Staff already using AI without a shared policy
- Unclear client-data, vendor, retention, and access boundaries
- Demo fatigue and fear of another abandoned pilot
- No practical metric or workflow owner for AI experimentation
- Concern that automation will amplify a broken or inconsistent process

## Objections

Likely objections include:

- “We already have an answering service, intake team, CRM, or case-management
  platform.”
- “Our workflow and data are too messy.”
- “This will become another pilot that staff do not use.”
- “AI cannot handle empathy, legal judgment, or a high-value claimant.”
- “We cannot expose client or medical data to another vendor.”
- “Integration will disrupt the team or create duplicate work.”
- “We have seen enough generic AI demos.”
- “The economics are unclear.”
- “Our current vendor says it is adding AI.”
- “We do not want a black box or vendor lock-in.”

Treat these as rational operating concerns, not resistance to innovation.

## Buying triggers

The strongest trigger is an observable change or event that makes a known pain
more urgent. Useful triggers include:

- hiring multiple intake, call-center, records, case-management, or lien roles;
- opening a new office, expanding geography, or increasing advertising;
- adding after-hours coverage, live chat, a new phone system, or a new intake
  vendor;
- migrating to Filevine, Lead Docket, CASEpeer, Clio, Litify, SmartAdvocate, or
  another modern system;
- a leader publicly discussing intake conversion, missed calls, follow-up,
  operational scale, staff workload, client communication, AI policy, or vendor
  risk;
- a podcast transcript describing a recurring workflow bottleneck or a growth
  priority in the speaker's own words;
- reviews that repeatedly mention responsiveness or communication, provided the
  pattern is material and fairly characterized;
- an association talk, webinar, article, or social post about modernizing the
  firm;
- a major growth event, acquisition, rebrand, leadership hire, or operating-role
  appointment; or
- an explicit request for recommendations, examples, benchmarks, or peers who
  have solved a similar problem.

## Questions that reveal fit

Research should try to answer these before recommending a lead:

- How do inquiries enter the firm: phone, form, chat, email, referral, or an
  answering service?
- What happens to a web form or missed call after hours?
- Who owns the time from inquiry to meaningful human contact?
- What happens after the first unsuccessful contact attempt?
- How are urgent, serious, or competitive matters escalated?
- How are retainer, signature, acceptance, and final disposition tracked?
- Which intake or case-management systems appear to be in use?
- Where does staff appear to reconstruct status manually?
- Which role is hiring, growing, or publicly talking about the problem?
- What has changed recently enough to make the issue timely?
- Is the evidence about this specific firm, a specific person, or only the
  broader market?

Questions for a later learning conversation should focus on concrete past
behavior:

- “What happened the last time a serious inquiry arrived after hours?”
- “Where did the last delayed handoff sit before someone noticed it?”
- “Which follow-up step still depends on someone remembering to do it?”
- “What did your team try the last time you attempted to improve this?”
- “What would have to be true for a narrow pilot to be worth keeping?”

## Customer language

Prefer the customer's operating vocabulary:

- signed cases, qualified opportunities, cost per signed case
- speed to lead, first meaningful human contact, contact rate
- intake window, after-hours gap, overflow, callback
- qualification, escalation, handoff, follow-up
- retainer completion, case acceptance, final disposition
- staff hours, backlog, cycle time, case velocity
- records outstanding, treatment gaps, demand readiness
- client updates, lien reductions, disbursement delay
- current stack, workflow owner, human review, audit trail

Avoid describing the customer as “behind,” “legacy,” “inefficient,” or “not
AI-ready.” Describe an observed workflow and its consequence without insulting
the people doing the work.

## Fears

- Losing a valuable case because another firm responded first
- Spending more on leads without improving signed-case conversion
- A machine mishandling an injured or emotionally vulnerable person
- Bad legal, ethical, privacy, or reputational consequences
- Staff resistance, shadow workflows, or duplicate data entry
- A costly implementation that never becomes part of daily work
- Losing control of firm-specific rules, data, or vendor relationships
- Automating inconsistent intake and making the inconsistency faster
- Being sold a polished demo that ignores the firm's real exceptions

## Decision criteria

A likely buyer will evaluate:

- Does this solve a real, costly workflow rather than showcase AI?
- Can the first use case be narrow, measurable, and safely piloted?
- Does it fit the people and systems already in place?
- Are human judgment and sensitive conversations clearly protected?
- Are data access, retention, vendor, and audit boundaries explicit?
- Is there credible proof that the team can operate production systems?
- Can the firm inspect, control, and improve the rules over time?
- Is there a practical metric: response time, conversion, cycle time, staff
  hours, client-update load, or risk reduction?
- Can the engagement stop after a diagnostic or pilot without becoming a
  sunk-cost trap?

## Disqualifiers and negative evidence

Lower priority or reject candidates when:

- the firm does little or no plaintiff-side PI work;
- there is no identifiable workflow fit beyond generic interest in AI;
- the recommended person has no connection to the problem or buying process;
- the firm appears too early, too low-volume, or too financially constrained for
  a scoped implementation;
- public evidence shows a strong in-house engineering and AI program already
  solving the exact workflow;
- the proposed angle depends on a privacy-invasive, deceptive, or unverified
  inference;
- the only signal is firm size, follower count, awards, or a broad “innovative”
  label; or
- the evidence is stale and no current trigger can be found.

## Evidence rules for the lead finder

### Source hierarchy

1. **Direct first-party evidence:** the firm's site, job posts, public talks,
   interviews, podcast transcripts, articles, and the person's own posts.
2. **Corroborated operating evidence:** multiple reviews, vendor directories,
   credible news, public technology signals, and association material.
3. **PossibleOS context:** existing firm/contact records, prior research, vendor
   signals, relationship history, and deduplication data.
4. **Reddit and forum material:** strong for learning language, objections, and
   market patterns; weak for firm-level attribution unless the identity and firm
   link are public and independently verified.

Mission Control podcast transcripts are especially useful when a named PI
leader describes a current priority or a past workflow in their own words. Quote
or closely paraphrase the relevant passage and preserve the episode reference.

### Required shape of a lead recommendation

Every recommendation should contain:

- person, role, firm, and why that role matters;
- verified source links or transcript references;
- the observed signal, separated from the agent's inference;
- one primary pain hypothesis and one possible conversation angle;
- why the issue may matter now;
- contrary or missing evidence;
- confidence: high, medium, or low; and
- PossibleOS match or deduplication status.

Never expose private PossibleOS relationship data as an outbound talking point
unless the user explicitly approves it. Never state a pain hypothesis as if the
firm confirmed it.
