## Why this file exists

Use this file to decide whether a candidate belongs in the Possible Minds lead
universe and which parts of the company are relevant to that candidate. Do not
turn every mention of AI, law, or automation into a lead.

## What the company sells

Possible Minds builds **diagnostic-led, AI-assisted operating systems for
personal injury firms**, beginning with intake revenue recovery.

The primary product wedge is an intake system that can:

- respond to phone, browser voice, and web inquiries;
- let an injured person describe what happened;
- organize the important facts into a structured intake record;
- identify priority and escalation signals under firm-approved rules;
- prepare the right human to respond; and
- preserve transcripts, recordings, execution traces, and handoff evidence.

The broader engagement is not a generic chatbot or a collection of disconnected
AI features. Possible Minds diagnoses a workflow, selects one narrow and
measurable use case, designs review and escalation rules, integrates it with the
firm's existing stack, pilots it, and helps the firm own the resulting operating
system.

The website also describes adjacent workflow systems for:

- case development, records, bills, treatment updates, and demand readiness;
- client communication and routine status updates;
- liens, settlement, and disbursement;
- email triage, routing, and response drafting;
- firm intelligence and operational visibility;
- AI readiness, vendor risk, data boundaries, and governance; and
- AI-search visibility and reputation diagnostics.

## Who it serves

### Primary market

US plaintiff-side personal injury firms, especially firms where lead flow,
intake execution, case progression, or vendor coordination materially affects
revenue.

The people most likely to buy or sponsor an engagement are:

- founder, owner, or managing partner;
- COO, executive director, or director of operations;
- intake director or call-center leader;
- marketing or growth leader accountable for lead conversion; and
- a firm administrator, systems owner, or legal-operations leader.

Operational staff such as intake specialists, case managers, records teams, and
lien or settlement coordinators are important pain witnesses and potential
champions. They should not automatically be treated as economic buyers.

### Secondary market

Possible Minds has production experience in regulated, high-volume healthcare
operations, including medical imaging workflows that serve PI firms. For the
first lead-finder loop, however, adjacent healthcare or legal vendors are
**research-only unless the user explicitly expands the target market**. The
default lead queue should remain focused on PI firms.

## How it makes money

The public website does not publish prices or a definitive commercial model.
The current working model, inferred from the engagement process, is:

1. A low-friction diagnostic identifies a costly workflow and a measurable
   baseline.
2. A paid, scoped engagement designs, builds, integrates, and pilots one bounded
   system.
3. Successful systems expand into additional workflows, monitoring, support,
   or a broader firm-owned operating layer.

Treat this as a **working assumption to confirm with the founder**, not a public
pricing claim. Never invent fees, contract terms, implementation timelines, or
subscription structure.

## What it believes

Possible Minds believes:

- Start with the leak, not the demo.
- Start with one workflow, one baseline, one owner, and one decision gate.
- Automate delay and repetitive coordination; keep empathy, persuasion, legal
  judgment, exceptions, and case acceptance with people.
- Work around the firm's current phones, intake tools, case-management system,
  and habits instead of demanding wholesale replacement.
- Design data boundaries, approved tools, review gates, escalation rules, and
  audit logs before sensitive production use.
- A useful pilot may end in rollout, iteration, or retirement. An inconclusive
  result is information, not a reason to force expansion.
- The durable product is firm-owned operating knowledge: rules, thresholds,
  handoffs, logs, and an improvement rhythm the firm can understand.
- PI is a strong starting market because contingency economics reward faster
  response, better follow-up, and fewer case delays.

## What makes it different

- **PI-specific operating context:** the company is building around intake
  economics, case progression, medical workflows, liens, vendors, and the
  human-judgment boundaries of plaintiff law.
- **Diagnosis before implementation:** it first identifies where value is
  leaking and whether automation is actually the right first move.
- **Cross-stack execution:** the long-term thesis is an execution layer across
  systems of record, not one isolated AI feature inside one vendor product.
- **Human-controlled operation:** sensitive decisions remain behind explicit
  human review and escalation gates.
- **Production systems, not demo theater:** the public site describes a live PI
  intake deployment and production automation experience in a 60-location
  medical imaging operation.
- **Observable systems:** transcripts, recordings, traces, tests, metrics, and
  audit logs are part of the product, not afterthoughts.
- **Firm ownership:** workflows, operating rules, and, in the AI Builder Program,
  source code are designed to remain understandable and controllable by the
  firm.

## Boundaries the lead finder must preserve

- Do not describe Possible Minds as replacing lawyers, intake closers, or staff.
- Do not present it as a general-purpose AI consultancy that will automate
  anything.
- Do not imply that it gives legal advice or decides whether a case is accepted.
- Do not claim guaranteed revenue, conversion, signed cases, or response by an
  attorney.
- Do not turn experience with one client into an unsupported industry-wide
  benchmark.
- Do not imply a partnership, endorsement, or data-sharing relationship merely
  because a firm appears in PossibleOS or has interacted with a known vendor.
- Do not recommend a lead solely because the firm is large, visible, or
  “tech-forward.” There must be a plausible workflow, economic, or risk fit.

## Lead-finder interpretation

The best candidate is not “a PI firm that likes AI.” It is a PI firm where
there is credible evidence of:

1. a meaningful workflow leak or operating constraint;
2. an owner or operator who feels the consequence;
3. enough volume or value for the leak to matter;
4. a bounded first workflow that Possible Minds can credibly address; and
5. a reason the issue may matter now.

The lead finder should recommend a person, a firm, the evidence, and a possible
conversation angle. It should not pretend that research has already proven the
pain or that the candidate has buying intent.
