# Offer

> **Status:** First draft for the Possible Minds lead-finder agent  
> **Last grounded:** 2026-08-23  
> **Important:** The website does not publish fixed implementation prices.

## Offer principle

Possible Minds does not begin by selling “AI transformation.” It begins by
finding a valuable workflow leak, establishing a baseline, and defining the
smallest safe system that could improve it.

The lead finder should use offers to choose a relevant **conversation angle**.
It should not claim that a candidate needs a product, quote a price, or write a
full sales pitch.

## Public entry offers

### Intake Revenue Audit

A 30-minute diagnostic for a PI firm's real intake path. The public page says it
aims to produce:

- a visible intake baseline;
- one prioritized revenue leak; and
- a bounded first workflow, including what remains human, which systems are
  involved, and how improvement would be measured.

There is no obligation to buy. Do not lead a first outbound message with the
word “free”; lead with the relevant finding or question.

### Intake Agent pilot

A working AI-assisted front door for phone, browser voice, and web inquiries.
Its current public capabilities include:

- immediate acknowledgment and story-first fact collection;
- structured intake records;
- priority and escalation signals;
- CRM collaboration and handoff evidence;
- optional acknowledgment-email preparation or sending under consent rules; and
- inspectable transcripts, recordings, traces, and behavioral tests.

Humans retain empathy, judgment, legal screening, representation, and case
acceptance.

### Diagnostic call and workflow engagement

The broader engagement path is stage-gated:

1. Diagnostic call
2. Workflow audit
3. Use-case selection
4. System design and governance plan
5. Build and integration
6. Limited pilot
7. Rollout, ownership, and improvement rhythm

Each stage should produce a decision and allow the firm to proceed, narrow,
pause, or stop.

### AI Builder Program for PI firm owners

A program for turning one recurring PI workflow into a narrow internal tool with
an AI coding agent. The firm keeps its current stack and the resulting code. The
program starts with one user, one input, and one verifiable result, using
synthetic or sanitized data before sensitive-data rules are established.

### Supporting diagnostic tools

- AI Readiness Audit
- AI Reputation / search-visibility diagnostic
- Ask Mira, an operational AI consultation for PI owners and operators

These are useful when the evidence points to AI policy, vendor risk, visibility,
or early-stage decision support rather than a confirmed intake leak.

## Working package ladder

These are **internal package categories**, not published fixed-price packages.

### 1. Diagnose

**Purpose:** Determine whether a real workflow is worth changing.

Possible deliverables:

- current-state workflow map;
- baseline metric and bottleneck;
- data-readiness and risk notes;
- recommended first use case; and
- a proceed, fix-prerequisites, narrow, or stop decision.

### 2. Build and pilot one bounded system

**Purpose:** Test one measurable workflow in the firm's actual operation.

Possible deliverables:

- implementation specification;
- approved data sources and vendor boundaries;
- instructions, review gates, escalation rules, and “must never decide” rules;
- a working integration path around current systems;
- test data, review surfaces, behavioral tests, logs, and audit evidence;
- a limited production pilot; and
- a pilot report with rollout, iteration, or retirement recommendation.

### 3. Operate and expand

**Purpose:** Turn a successful workflow into a firm-owned operating capability.

Possible deliverables:

- staff training and documented ownership;
- monitoring, quality review, and improvement rhythm;
- reusable integration and deployment capability; and
- expansion into the next high-value workflow.

Do not present recurring support, monitoring, or expansion terms as fixed until
the founder confirms the commercial model.

## Pricing logic

No public price list was found. Until a pricing source of truth exists, the lead
finder must follow these rules:

- Never invent a dollar amount, implementation duration, discount, ROI, or
  payback period.
- Treat price as a function of workflow scope, integrations, volume, data risk,
  review requirements, pilot design, and ongoing support.
- Anchor the conversation in the cost of the observed leak and the value of a
  measurable improvement, not in generic software-seat comparisons.
- Keep diagnostic, pilot, and expansion economics separate.
- Mark pricing and packaging as **to be confirmed by the user**.

## Promises the company can credibly make

- Start with one real workflow and a practical metric.
- Say when AI does not fit or when process cleanup should come first.
- Design around the firm's current people, systems, and operating rules.
- Keep legal judgment and sensitive client decisions with people.
- Make data boundaries, review gates, escalation, and auditability explicit.
- Give the firm a decision at each engagement gate.
- Build for operational use and inspection, not merely a polished demo.

## Claims to avoid

Never claim or imply:

- guaranteed signed cases, conversion, revenue, settlement value, or ROI;
- that a lawyer or intake closer will always respond within a specific time;
- 100% accuracy, zero hallucinations, zero risk, or universal integration;
- that AI replaces staff, makes legal judgments, gives legal advice, or accepts
  cases;
- regulatory, bar-rule, HIPAA, privacy, or security compliance unless a current
  approved source supports the exact claim;
- that Possible Minds is endorsed by a firm, vendor, client, or association;
- fixed pricing, fixed delivery time, or a standard subscription not published
  by the company;
- results from previously removed case-study metrics;
- access to a firm's private data, vendor history, or internal workflow; or
- a diagnosed problem based only on an inference.

Avoid “bespoke AI agents” as the first description. It is broad, difficult to
price mentally, and sounds like a custom-technology project before the buyer has
seen a specific operational reason to care.

## What counts as a good-fit customer

A good-fit customer:

- has a costly or high-volume workflow that can be clearly mapped;
- can name or estimate a baseline and a practical success metric;
- has an accountable workflow owner;
- wants to keep humans on empathy, judgment, and exceptions;
- will define data, access, review, and escalation boundaries;
- is willing to begin with a limited scope and real operating feedback;
- prefers integration with the current stack over a disruptive replacement; and
- can decide after the pilot whether to roll out, iterate, or stop.

A poor fit wants a magical general agent, immediate full-firm transformation,
unreviewed legal decision-making, or guaranteed results without workflow access,
ownership, or measurement.

## Evidence-to-angle map

| Observed evidence | Primary hypothesis | Suggested entry angle |
|---|---|---|
| After-hours intake hiring, voicemail complaints, or leader discussion of missed calls | Serious inquiries may lose momentum outside staffed windows | Map one after-hours inquiry path; consider Intake Revenue Audit |
| Heavy advertising, multiple offices, intake growth, or conversion discussion | Lead spend may be leaking between inquiry and signed case | Intake baseline, escalation, follow-up, and attribution |
| Intake or call-center hiring and churn | Volume or inconsistency may be creating queue and handoff pressure | One narrow intake workflow with a named owner and metric |
| Modern CRM/CMS migration | The firm may have a new system of record but unresolved work between systems | Cross-stack workflow audit, not system replacement |
| Records, treatment, demand, or case-delay discussion | Manual chase may be slowing case readiness | Case-development diagnostic; quantify cycle time or staff hours |
| Client-update complaints or client-service hiring | Routine status work may be creating staff drag and client anxiety | Grounded update workflow with clear human escalation |
| Lien or disbursement discussion | Provider follow-up and reductions may delay closing | Lien/settlement workflow map |
| Staff AI use, policy questions, or vendor-risk discussion | Adoption may be ahead of governance | AI Readiness or vendor-risk diagnostic |
| AI-search or reputation concern | Visibility and trust may be limiting acquisition | Reputation/search diagnostic |
| Owner names a recurring tool they wish existed | Firm-specific exceptions may justify a narrow internal tool | AI Builder Program |

The evidence earns a question, not a conclusion. Phrase recommendations as
“possible angle” and explain what still needs to be validated.
